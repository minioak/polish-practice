Sound pairs

The contrasts that trip learners up: sounds that are easy to mix up, and sounds that are spelled two ways.

Sounds

What to listen for

Examples

s · sz · ś
/s/ · /ʂ/ · /ɕ/
Three different sounds: s is a plain hiss, sz is hard with the tongue back, ś is soft with the tongue flat.
kasa — till, kasza — groats, Kasia — Kate
z · ż · ź
/z/ · /ʐ/ · /ʑ/
The voiced partners of s · sz · ś: plain, hard and soft.
zupa — soup, żaba — frog, źle — badly
c · cz · ć
/t͡s/ · /t͡ʂ/ · /t͡ɕ/
"ts", hard "ch" and soft "ch".
co — what, czas — time, ćma — moth
dz · dż · dź
/d͡z/ · /d͡ʐ/ · /d͡ʑ/
"ds", hard "j" and soft "j".
dzwon — bell, dżem — jam, dźwięk — sound
i · y
/i/ · /ɨ/
i is a short "ee"; y is like "i" in "bit" with the tongue pulled back. Swapping them changes the word.
bić — to beat, być — to be
l · ł
/l/ · /w/
l is a clear English l; ł sounds like English w.
lata — years, łata — patch
ż = rz
/ʐ/
One sound, two spellings. You just learn which words use which.
może — maybe, morze — sea
u = ó
/u/
One sound, two spellings. ó often swaps with o in related words (stół, stoły).
ul — beehive, ból — pain
h = ch
/x/
One sound, two spellings: a rough breath like Scottish "loch".
hałas — noise, chleb — bread
ą · ę
/ɔ̃/ · /ɛ̃/
Nasal vowels. Before b/p they sound like om/em, before t/d/c/cz like on/en, and final ę is usually a plain e.
ząb — tooth (say "zomp"), ręka — hand (say "renka")
chleb · róg
/p/ · /k/
At the end of a word voiced consonants lose their voice: b→p, d→t, g→k, w→f, z→s, ż/rz→sz.
chleb — bread (say "hlep"), róg — corner (say "rook")
